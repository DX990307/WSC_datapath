\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Overall Performance}

Figure~\ref{fig:cupath-performance} compares CuPath with Baseline across the
fourteen workloads. CuPath achieves a 1.492$\times$ geometric-mean speedup.
MM, FWS, and KM obtain the largest gains at 6.180$\times$, 4.341$\times$,
and 2.501$\times$, respectively. Local workloads also benefit. BT and FWT
improve by 1.635$\times$ and 1.645$\times$, while RELU improves by
1.149$\times$. I2C improves by 1.215$\times$ through the remote path. Eight
workloads improve by more than 1\%, while the remaining six stay within 1\%
of Baseline. SPMV is the only strict regression and remains within 0.1\% of
Baseline. CuPath therefore provides large gains when the memory path exposes
pairing, aggregation, or reuse opportunities without materially penalizing
workloads that expose few such opportunities.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

Figure~\ref{fig:cupath-ablation} isolates the three CuPath transformations.
Remote Aggregation is the strongest standalone transformation and achieves a
1.209$\times$ geometric-mean speedup. Remote Reuse and Local Pairing achieve
1.156$\times$ and 1.108$\times$, respectively. The different geometric means
reflect the opportunity exposed at each memory-path stage rather than a
uniform benefit across all workloads. Remote Aggregation is most effective
when remote reads expose same-line duplication or same-page grouping. FWS and
KM consequently reach 2.222$\times$ and 2.462$\times$. Remote Reuse is more concentrated and
provides its largest gain on MM at 5.451$\times$, where later requests can be
served by the requesting-GPM L2 instead of crossing the wafer again. I2C also
benefits from Remote Reuse at 1.097$\times$.

Local Pairing targets a different request population. BT and FWT improve by
1.635$\times$ and 1.645$\times$, and RELU improves by 1.149$\times$. For these
workloads, the complete configuration closely follows Local Pairing because
little remote traffic reaches the other two transformations. In contrast,
workloads that expose fewer usable pairing opportunities remain close to
Baseline. The standalone results therefore show that no single transformation
explains CuPath's gains across the complete workload set.

Complete CuPath reaches 1.492$\times$ and exceeds every standalone
configuration in geometric mean. Workloads exposing several opportunities
benefit from their composition. FWS improves from 2.222$\times$ with Remote
Aggregation to 4.341$\times$ with Complete. MM combines a 2.341$\times$
Remote Aggregation result with a 5.451$\times$ Remote Reuse result and reaches
6.180$\times$ with Complete. I2C reaches 1.215$\times$, compared with
1.097$\times$ for its strongest standalone configuration. When one
opportunity dominates, Complete remains close to the corresponding standalone
result. KM, for example, reaches 2.501$\times$ with Complete and
2.462$\times$ with Remote Aggregation. The speedups are complementary but not
additive because every stage operates on the requests that remain after
earlier stages have removed or combined work.

\subsection{Large-Model Workloads}

Figure~\ref{fig:cupath-large-models} evaluates CuPath on BERT, GPT, and
ResNet.  CuPath accelerates all three workloads, with substantially larger
gains for BERT and GPT than for ResNet.  Transformer execution repeatedly
exposes adjacent accesses, concurrent remote requests, and remote data reuse,
allowing CuPath to remove or combine work at several points along the memory
path.  ResNet exposes fewer of these opportunities, but still benefits from
the same mechanisms.  The results show that CuPath extends beyond traditional
GPU kernels and remains effective across both transformer and convolutional
workloads.

\subsection{Removing Work Along the Path}

Figure~\ref{fig:cupath-work} explains the speedup in terms of work that no
longer reaches a downstream component. Same-line deduplication is the number
of logical remote reads merged into an existing transaction divided by all
logical remote reads. Same-page packet reduction is the difference between
post-dedup cacheline transactions and request packets divided by the former.
Repeated-traversal elimination counts logical remote reads served by the
requesting-GPM L2 Cache, normalized by all logical remote reads. The weighted
bars sum the corresponding numerator and denominator across all workloads.
Complete deduplicates 9.4\% of remote reads, reduces remote request packets by
52.7\%, and eliminates 29.4\% of repeated wafer traversals.

CuPath does not obtain these gains by increasing aggregate memory traffic.
Complete reduces physical DRAM reads from 39.25 to 38.71 million (1.38\%) and
writes from 20.83 to 20.68 million (0.71\%).  The sample-weighted local
L2-miss-to-DRAM-issue latency falls from 38.12~ns in Baseline to 20.34~ns over
the ordinary and Filter-negative Complete issue populations.  We report these
populations separately in the machine-readable results; the comparison is not
an assumption that every miss takes the fast path.

\subsection{Typed Filter Effectiveness}

Figure~\ref{fig:filter-effectiveness} shows how often a negative Filter result
eliminates an exact metadata lookup in each CuPath application. \emph{Remote
Aggregation} reports remote reads that bypass the requester-GPM RDMA
request-table search. \emph{Remote Reuse} reports remote reads that bypass the
requester-GPM L2 Cache lookup because the requested line is not retained there.
\emph{Local Pairing} reports local reads that bypass the local-GPM L2 Cache
lookup and proceed directly to miss handling.
\emph{Average} is calculated from the total bypasses and opportunities across
all workloads rather than from the arithmetic mean of workload percentages.

Remote Aggregation safely bypasses 89.57 million requester-RDMA table searches,
or 90.6\% of all opportunities. The rate exceeds 84\% for I2C, KM, MM, MT, PR,
and SPMV, showing that most remote reads do not require an exact search before
a new transaction is created. Remote Reuse bypasses 13.27 million requester-GPM
L2 Cache lookups, corresponding to an aggregate rate of 27.8\%. The rate
reaches 53.0\% for FWS, 98.4\% for MT, and 99.8\% for SPMV. Missing remote
bars indicate that the workload generates no qualifying remote access. Local
Pairing bypasses 32.61 million local-GPM L2 Cache lookups, or 24.1\% of all
opportunities. The rate exceeds 97\% for AES, FWT, FFT, and RELU, showing that
most Filter-eligible reads in these workloads are absent from L2 Cache. Only
65 apparent false negatives occur across all workloads, all during short L2
Cache write-update windows.

\subsection{Local Pairing Effectiveness}

Figure~\ref{fig:local-pairing-diagnostics} evaluates the requests issued by
Local Pairing. Accuracy is the fraction of issued pairs whose adjacent line is
later demanded. Coverage is the fraction of local read-miss lines served by an
issued adjacent line. Timeliness is the fraction of useful adjacent lines that
return to the staging buffer before the demand arrives. Across all workloads,
82.2\% of issued pairs are useful and cover 12.4\% of local read misses. 10.3\%
of useful lines are timely, while the remaining useful lines join their paired
access in flight.

Classified unused lines account for 17.5\% of issued pairs. Their 66.2~MB of
data correspond to 2.65\% of the demand-miss bytes. After useful paired lines
replace later singleton reads, total HBM read traffic increases by 30.0~MB, or
1.17\% over Baseline, while aggregate HBM row activations decrease by 10.1\%.
The staging buffer supplies 500,125 demanded lines and evicts 458,140 unused
lines. The normalized bars report both events per 1,000 issued pairs.

\subsection{M2: Cuckoo-Filter-Guided Remote Aggregation}

M2 is the largest standalone contributor.  Complete records 4.24 million
real demands merged by exact RDMA line state and 2.52 million predicted lines
piggybacked into existing home-GPM/page packets.  Because a real demand can take
over a candidate before batch insertion, the aggregate counters do not expose
the intersection of these two populations; we therefore do not relabel every
useful prediction as an avoided packet.  The exact merge and wire-packet
counters independently establish the reductions.

\subsection{M3: Cuckoo-Filter-Guided Remote Reuse}

M3 bypasses 23.41 million exact requesting-GPM L2 probes when \textsc{Resident}
and \textsc{Seen} reliably prove absence, 42.6\% of eligible unique-request
probe opportunities.  The remaining probes produce 27.23 million physical
L2 hits and satisfy 28.21 million logical responses through exact waiter
fanout.  M3 installs 7.06 million remote-clean lines in the existing L2;
0.996 million are observed to retire unused, a 14.1\% lower bound because
lines still resident at termination are unclassified.  Invalid ways absorb
5.35 million fills and older remote-clean lines absorb the remaining 1.71
million; no ordinary local-clean line is displaced.  The largest
workload-level sum of peak occupancies reaches 37.5\% of wafer L2 lines, a
conservative non-simultaneous upper bound rather than an average footprint.
Of the unused retirements, 160,257 carry candidate provenance and remove the
associated \textsc{Pattern}.  A useful patterned hit instead keeps
\textsc{Pattern} eligible; the aggregate counter does not isolate that subset
from all requesting-GPM L2 hits.

\subsection{Sensitivity Study}

Figures~\ref{fig:cupath-sensitivity-filter}--\ref{fig:cupath-sensitivity-l2}
evaluate CuPath's sensitivity to the typed Cuckoo Filter capacity, L2 Cache
capacity, and the number of cachelines covered by one row-local HBM access.
Every Complete configuration is normalized to the same fixed Baseline used by
the overall-performance study, and each panel reports all fourteen workloads
followed by their geometric mean.  Keeping one denominator makes the absolute
performance of different configurations directly comparable.  For the L2
sweep, this normalization measures end-to-end sensitivity to cache capacity
rather than CuPath's incremental benefit over a capacity-matched Baseline.

CuPath is largely insensitive to Filter capacity.  Each sweep label gives the
aggregate number of buckets/GPM distributed across four physical arrays; the
32K point matches the overall-performance configuration. Increasing capacity
from 4K to 32K buckets per GPM produces geometric-mean speedups of
1.474$\times$, 1.471$\times$, 1.470$\times$, and 1.479$\times$.  The entire
range remains within 0.6\% of the 16K-bucket configuration, showing that the
Filter does not rely on overprovisioned metadata capacity to retain its
performance benefit.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../../akkalat/results/2026-07-25-19-41-19-cupath-sensitivity/cupath_filter_capacity_sensitivity.pdf}
  \caption{Sensitivity to aggregate typed Cuckoo Filter capacity per GPM,
  distributed across four physical arrays and normalized to the fixed
  overall-performance Baseline. Bars above 2$\times$ are clipped.}
  \label{fig:cupath-sensitivity-filter}
\end{figure}

The row-local HBM region has a clearer optimum.  Regions containing 2, 4, 8,
and 16 cachelines achieve 1.470$\times$, 1.428$\times$, 1.351$\times$, and
1.237$\times$, respectively.  A two-cacheline region captures the recurring
adjacent access while limiting unnecessary data movement.  Expanding the
region fetches progressively more lines whose use is less certain and consumes
additional HBM and cache resources.  The decreasing speedup therefore supports
CuPath's adjacent-line design.

L2 capacities of 1, 2, and 4~MiB achieve geometric-mean speedups of
1.249$\times$, 1.448$\times$, and 1.470$\times$, respectively, across all

\subsection{Limitations}

The long runs expose a limitation hidden by the short screen. Across the
fourteen Complete runs, active residency, RDMA, and paired-access state have
zero insertion failures. Capacity pressure instead rejects 5.57 million of
13.17 million low-priority remote-history insertions and 2.85 million of
27.59 million demand-pattern insertions. These failures suppress optional
reuse or pairing and cannot change demand correctness, but they bound
coverage. The modest
1.187$\times$ Remote-group speedup and M1's FIR regression likewise show that
CuPath is constrained by available aggregation and safe idle opportunities,
not by an unlimited speculative upper bound.

A controlled simulator-runtime screen uses seven representative workloads,
all five configurations, 192 drained workgroups, one host worker, and the
same binary. Normalized by simulated GPU time, Complete costs
1.190$\times$ Baseline. This value is simulator overhead rather than hardware
performance.
